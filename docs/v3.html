Fly-Brain v3 Test

Version 3 adds news senses with a keyword crawler, a toolkit of simple maths models, a "maths school" with exams, a second brain that decides long or short, and love for beating the S&P 500. It traded five markets, including Moscow, on 2024–26 data it had never seen. Everything is measured in US dollars against the S&P 500.

Verdict
It didn't beat the S&P 500 in any market, and no edge survives correction for multiple tests.

The S&P 500 returned about 20–22% a year over the test. The fly's best market was Moscow at a year, then Tokyo at . Its Tokyo stock picks again beat random picks (p = raw). That is the second Tokyo test where this has shown up, but it falls to after correcting for 10 tests.

Pre-registered hypothesis tests

The numbers behind the verdict

Beats S&P: one-sided Newey–West test on weekly dollar returns minus the S&P 500. Stock-picking edge: the fly against 2,000 random pickers making the same number of trades, with the same long/short timing. Timing edge: the fly's long/short calls against the same calls moved to random weeks. Corrected p-values use the Holm method (5 tests for "beats S&P", 10 for the edges).

Maths school · report card

It passed three of five exams, and failed the two most basic ones

Before seeing real prices, the fly trained on synthetic markets that each follow one textbook model, then sat an exam on a fresh market of each kind. The score is the rank correlation between its stock scores and next week's returns. The "best possible" column is the score you'd get by knowing the hidden true signal; the pass mark was half of that. It handled low volatility and news, and correctly found nothing in a random market. It never got good at momentum or mean reversion, even with a direct sensory pathway, regime senses and up to 6 rounds of lessons. The likely reason is that the two rules contradict each other ("buy winners" vs "buy losers"), and its simple learning rule can't keep both.

What's inside v3

How the new pieces work

News senses and crawler

For training and the blind test, the fly read the GDELT world-news archive (65 languages, machine-translated) from 2017 to 2026. Each day it got four numbers per market: article volume, average tone, and the share of articles containing bullish words (rally, buyback, upgrade, record…) or bearish words (selloff, sanctions, downgrade, probe…). It only used news up to the previous day. For live use, niche/crawler.py scans the last 24 hours of headlines for the same keyword lists, so live senses match the training senses.

Simple models ("maths basics")

Six textbook models feed the stock brain as extra senses: a 50/200-day trend, 12-month time-series momentum, a Bollinger mean-reversion z-score, a rolling least-squares forecast refitted weekly on the past two years, EWMA volatility and market beta. Two regime senses tell it which basic model has been paying lately.

Long or short

A second, smaller mushroom body reads market news, the market's trend and the S&P 500's trend each week. When it is much more bearish than usual, the fly goes short. On MOEX it shorts the stock brain's bottom picks, paying a 3% a year borrow fee. Elsewhere it shorts the whole market as an index future would. It was short in 1–15% of weeks.

Love

Every week the fly compares its last 52 weeks of dollar returns with the S&P 500's. When it's ahead, it feels love in proportion to the lead (full strength at 10 points ahead). Love strengthens the synapses behind whatever it is holding and its current long/short call. Turning love off changed yearly returns by between −0.1 and +6 points depending on the market, and none of those differences is statistically clear.

Limits and disclosures

  • MOEX is research only. The UK sanctioned the Moscow Exchange in June 2024, so a UK person can't trade there. This used public historical prices only.
  • News is market-level only in the backtest. The news archive throttles this cloud server, and per-company history for ~40 Russian firms would have taken many hours. The stock brain's company-news senses were therefore switched off (zero) in training and testing. Per-company news exists only in the live crawler.
  • Survivorship: stocks come from today's listings. For MOEX that means today's index members, which flatters it. The random pickers share the same lists, so the edge tests are fairer than the raw returns.
  • MOEX dividends were added back using registry dates from dohod.ru, with ex-dates inferred from the T+2/T+1 settlement rules. Splits were detected from price jumps above 3×.
  • Bug fix: the "news off" comparison crashed on the first full run. It was fixed and everything was rerun from scratch with the same locked settings. The headline numbers were identical.
  • Settings tuned on 2022–23: only how bearish the market brain must be before shorting (2 options). Both options trailed the S&P 500 in validation.
